# V. SUMMARY, CONCLUSIONS, AND RECOMMENDATIONS

## 5.1 Summary of Findings

This study developed GroomShield, an AI-powered moderation module combining contextual language analysis with behavioral sequence modeling to identify conversations missed by keyword and static-score approaches. DistilBERT evaluates each turn in its preceding context, and an LSTM processes seven chronological trajectory features. PAN-2012 was selected to evaluate the architecture using connected-author partitions, training-only derived resources, and a common conversation-level endpoint. The revised F2 analysis retains the trained models and applies validation-selected cutoffs to preserved test scores, as specified in Section 3.5.2.

The principal findings are:

1. The keyword rule detected 27 of 44 positive test conversations, achieving 0.6136 recall with 17 false negatives. Maximum Layer 1 aggregation detected 39 positives, achieving 0.8864 recall with five false negatives but 78 false positives. These results illustrate the coverage limits of lexical rules and the false-alert cost of expanding coverage through maximum-score aggregation alone.
2. The Trajectory-Feature LSTM achieved 0.9153 PR-AUC, 0.9930 ROC-AUC, 0.8613 F2, 0.6613 precision, 0.9318 recall, and 0.9884 specificity. It detected 41 of 44 positives and produced 21 false positives among 1,818 negatives.
3. The Trajectory-Feature LSTM reduced false negatives to three: 82.4% fewer than the keyword rule, 40.0% fewer than maximum Layer 1 aggregation, and 50.0% fewer than the weighted scorer. These are observed count reductions at independently validation-selected operating points.
4. The Trajectory-Feature LSTM exceeded the matched weighted scorer by 0.1103 PR-AUC and 0.0630 F2. Both paired 95% intervals were above zero, supporting the predictive contribution of recurrent aggregation. The separate recall-difference interval included zero.
5. The Embedding-and-Feature LSTM detected all 44 positives, with 0.8429 F2, 0.5176 precision, and 41 false positives. The trajectory-feature model's precision advantage was supported by the paired interval, while F2 and PR-AUC differences between the LSTMs were inconclusive. The trajectory-feature recurrent classifier also used 84.8% fewer trainable parameters.
6. The trajectory-feature model's three missed positives contained 2, 11, and 26 turns, all below the first quartile of correctly detected positive conversation lengths. This identifies short-history assessment as a focused direction for further evaluation; it does not independently measure grooming onset.
7. In a local CPU benchmark of the complete HTTP scoring path, the Trajectory-Feature and Embedding-and-Feature LSTMs processed 3.39 and 3.32 sequential requests per second, respectively, across an equal mixture of 1-, 4-, 8-, and 16-turn synthetic prefixes. Their pooled median latencies were 266.6 and 245.6 ms; p95 values were 708.8 and 717.7 ms, and p99 values were 829.8 and 1058.5 ms. Prefix recomputation caused longer chats to take longer. These are local prototype measurements, not production service-level guarantees.

## 5.2 Conclusions

The conclusions directly answer the General Objective and three Specific Objectives of the study:

1. **Specific Objective 1 - Evaluate existing keyword-based and rule-based moderation approaches.** Lexical matching and maximum contextual-score aggregation do not capture the same detection tradeoff as sequence modeling. The keyword rule missed 17 positives. Maximum Layer 1 aggregation reduced that count to five but generated 78 false positives, compared with three misses and 21 false positives for the Trajectory-Feature LSTM. Contextual scores alone therefore did not obtain the strongest measured F2 performance.
2. **Specific Objective 2 - Design and develop the AI-based moderation module.** The study successfully implemented GroomShield as a complete two-layer module combining context-conditioned DistilBERT scoring, seven interpretable trajectory features, LSTM-based sequence modeling, and a working interface for sequential conversation assessment. The final output is a conversation-level flag for human review.
3. **Specific Objective 3 - Assess detection improvement and false-negative reduction.** GroomShield achieved 93.18% recall with the Trajectory-Feature LSTM and 100% recall with the Embedding-and-Feature LSTM on the test partition. The trajectory-feature variant reduced observed false negatives by 82.4% relative to the keyword rule, 40.0% relative to maximum Layer 1 aggregation, and 50.0% relative to the weighted scorer; the embedding-and-feature variant missed none of the 44 positives, with 41 false alerts compared with 21 for the trajectory-feature variant. The supported F2 and PR-AUC advantages of the trajectory-feature variant over the matched weighted scorer establish the contribution of recurrent aggregation using the same seven inputs. F2 aligns the operating policy with reducing missed positives while retaining precision. Quantitative improvement is demonstrated against the tested keyword and score-based approaches; complementing user reporting is an intended application rather than a measured comparison with a deployed reporting system.
4. **General Objective - Develop an AI-powered moderation module that enhances existing chat moderation.** The study achieved this objective by developing and evaluating a contextual and behavioral sequence module that expands conversation-level detection coverage. The Trajectory-Feature LSTM offers the highest observed F2 among the evaluated methods, while the embedding-and-feature variant provides additional coverage at a greater false-alert cost. These findings support GroomShield as a technical foundation for complementing existing safeguards and human judgment.

## 5.3 Recommendations

Based on the results and remaining evaluation needs, the study recommends:

1. Evaluate the selected F2 operating policy on an independent external corpus with thresholds fixed before evaluation, testing cross-platform transfer and providing a fresh assessment of the revised policy.
2. Investigate short-history performance through controlled conversation-prefix evaluation. Separately develop expert-reviewed message, stage, and first-onset annotations before evaluating grooming onset or time to detection.
3. Conduct feature-removal, ordering, and architecture ablations to quantify the contribution of individual trajectory features and sequence-learning components.
4. Extend the local latency benchmark to representative traffic, longer conversation prefixes, concurrent requests, deployment hardware, memory use, network conditions, and human-review outcomes. Evaluate caching or incremental computation to avoid rescoring the entire prefix on each turn. Retain recall, false negatives, precision, and false positives as joint measures; select operational escalation policies using development data rather than retuning on evaluation outcomes.
5. Acquire representative contemporary and Philippine-context data, including Filipino and Taglish conversations where ethically and legally permissible, with privacy, fairness, and subgroup analysis.
6. Extend preprocessing and evaluation to multi-party conversations through a separately specified protocol preserving participant boundaries and preventing author leakage.
